Glance

Scan someone's badge or social QR, and Glance builds one profile for that person, adds a photo and a voice note of what you talked about, tags the event automatically, and turns it all into follow-ups you can act on after IBW, Devcon and ETHGlobal.

Problem & goals

At a conference week you meet 50 to 150 people in loud rooms. You connect on X, Telegram or LinkedIn, often through pseudonymous handles, and a week later you can't tell who @degenwizard was, what you discussed, or what you promised.

Goals

  • Capture a person in under 20 seconds: scan, photo, voice note.
  • Give each person one identity across X, LinkedIn, Telegram and WhatsApp, with suggestions for other platforms that you confirm yourself.
  • Glance context: face, event, place, what was said and what happens next.
  • Make follow-ups easy: each action has a drafted message and opens the right app.
  • Run locally with no backend and pay per use, about $0.10 or less per person.

Non-goals for v1

  • Sending DMs or following people automatically. You tap Follow and Send yourself.
  • Sync across devices, accounts, Android, or an App Store release.
  • Logging into your social accounts through OAuth (reasons in Your accounts).

Meeting people: what actually happens

One-on-one (the easy case)

  1. Open (0–10s): "Hey, what are you building?" Glance at the badge for name and company.
  2. Talk (2–8 min): what they do, what they need, any overlap with you.
  3. Exchange (10s): "Are you on Telegram?" One of you shows a QR. You scan theirs rather than having them scan yours, because their scan of you doesn't give Glance anything.
  4. Photo (5s, optional): "Quick selfie so I remember?" People in crypto often say no, so the badge photo is the fallback.
  5. After you part (30–60s): record the voice note while walking away. This is the step that gets skipped, so the app nags: a stub with no note gets a nudge 2 minutes later.

The rush (the real case)

After a talk or at a side event, 3 to 8 people crowd around and the conversation jumps between them. You can't record a note for each one, and pulling out the phone eight times is awkward. Rush mode is built for this:

  • Stubs first: the scanner stays open and every scan makes a stub with a timestamp and a beep, with no screens in between. "+ wave" saves someone you only have a face for, so it only needs a photo.
  • Star the ones that matter: one tap ★ marks a stub as important. This is the only judgement you make during the rush.
  • One note for the group: after the huddle, one voice note covers everyone ("Karan's the auditor, Marco does BD…"). Claude splits it per person by matching names, companies and handles to the stubs.
  • Group photo: one photo of the group. You tap faces to assign them to stubs later, at night, not in the moment.
  • When there's no time to scan: take one wide photo with the badges in view. Claude reads every badge it can see and creates a stub for each.
  • Hands-free start: on an iPhone 13, set up Back Tap (Settings → Accessibility → Touch → Back Tap) to run a Shortcut that opens glance://note. Double-tap the back of the phone in your pocket and it starts recording.

Letting the people who don't matter drop off

  • A stub with no star and no note only gets the free lookups (X, Telegram, ENS). No paid LinkedIn or search calls. That keeps costs down automatically.
  • The nightly recap lists those stubs as "Keep or drop?" and you swipe through them in about 30 seconds.
  • Anything not kept after 72 hours is archived, not deleted, so you can search for it later ("who was that person in the blue shirt?").
  • Stubs mentioned in a group note are promoted automatically. People you never mentioned are flagged for dropping (screen 15).

Decoding an unknown QR

When a QR isn't a known social link, Glance works out what it contains step by step. It reads the content as text and never opens the link in a browser or wallet, because a QR at a crypto event is an easy way to phish someone.

Identity resolution

The account you scanned is always the anchor. The app then looks for the same person elsewhere, using free sources first and paid ones only if needed. Every result is a suggestion with a reason until you confirm it.

Scan→Parse type→Fetch anchor profile→Extract links from bio / website (free)→ENS text records (free)→Search by name + company (paid)→Score + suggest→You confirm

Every profile keeps each platform's version

People often use a real photo on LinkedIn, a cartoon on X and a different name on Telegram. Glance stores each platform's photo, display name and bio separately, labelled with where it came from, and shows them side by side (screen 13). The profile shows the photo you took first, then LinkedIn's, then the others.

Confirmation rule

Nothing is ever linked automatically. Every handle except the one you scanned starts as suggested and only becomes confirmed when you tap it. Rejected candidates are remembered so they aren't suggested again.

Job queue

Every capture writes rows first and queues jobs in a jobs table: enrich_anchor, find_related, transcribe, structure. A single worker runs them when the app is in the foreground and online, retrying with backoff. That's enough. No background fetch or push is needed.

Your accounts

You asked for the app to connect to your social accounts. I recommend not using OAuth and using deep links plus your own handles instead:

  • X: the follow and DM APIs are paid write access, and twitterapi.io writes need your login cookie, which risks your account. Deep links cost nothing: x.com/intent/follow?screen_name= and x.com/messages/compose?recipient_id=&text=, which prefills the message.
  • LinkedIn: no public API allows connection requests or messages. Open the profile with linkedin:// and copy the drafted text to the clipboard.
  • Telegram: no user OAuth for DMs. Use tg://resolve?domain=<user> to open the chat, with the draft on the clipboard.
  • WhatsApp: whatsapp://send?phone=&text= prefills the message when you have the number.

"Connecting your accounts" then means entering your own handles once in Settings. That gives you the My card QR and lets drafts mention the right links.

Risks & open questions

  • 7-day signing: with a free Apple ID the app stops opening after 7 days, and the week of events is longer than that. Either pay for the Apple Developer Program ($99/yr) or plan to reinstall from your Mac around Nov 6. Turn on Developer Mode on the iPhone before Oct 23.
  • Badge format unknown: if IBW and Devcon badges only carry ticket IDs, the badge photo plus vision is the main path. Test this on day 1.
  • Wallet on the phone: keep it small ($20) with a daily cap. It's a hot wallet in the Keychain.
  • Other people's data: keep it on the device, only fetch public profiles, and don't use phone or address lookups (Whitepages, PDL contact data). LinkedIn data from third-party scrapers is a grey area under LinkedIn's terms, so use it only for your own notes.
  • Mumbai venues: expect weak signal indoors. The offline queue is a P0 requirement for this reason.
  • Wrong matches: a confident false match is worse than none. That's why suggestions are never linked automatically and low-confidence matches stay collapsed.
